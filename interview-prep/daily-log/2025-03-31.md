Focus: design + behavioral. 2 questions worked through this session.

### Q1. What is a write-ahead log (WAL) and why is it important?

**Answer.** Every change is appended to a durable log before being applied to the data structures; crash recovery replays the log (redo) and rolls back uncommitted work (undo). It's what makes a database durable after a power loss, and the same log powers replication, point-in-time recovery, and compaction in LSM stores.

---

### Q2. What does ACID stand for and which level do NoSQL systems relax?

**Answer.** Atomicity (all-or-nothing transactions), Consistency (state transitions preserve invariants), Isolation (concurrent transactions don't interfere), Durability (committed data survives crashes). NoSQL systems like Cassandra sacrifice strong consistency/isolation (offer tunable levels, e.g. QUORUM) for availability and horizontal scale.
