# Daily Interview Prep — April 03, 2025

Focus: data structures. 4 questions worked through this session.

---

### Q1. What is an index, and why aren't more indexes always better?

**Answer.** A B+tree (typically) mapping key columns to row pointers, turning O(n) scans into O(log n) lookups. Composite indexes follow the leftmost-prefix rule. Costs: every index is written on INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE (write amplification, extra disk), and the planner can pick a bad index. Index for your actual queries, measured via EXPLAIN.

---

### Q2. What is a covering index?

**Answer.** An index that contains all columns the query needs (e.g. INCLUDE in PostgreSQL, or a composite wide enough), so the query is answered from the index alone — no heap 'bookmark lookups'. Common in OLTP for 'index-only scans' on hot read paths.

---

### Q3. Explain a B+tree index.

**Answer.** All data (or row pointers) lives in the leaf level, leaves are linked for range scans, and interior nodes are pure routing keys. Balanced and shallow (3-4 levels for hundreds of millions of rows). The leaf linked list makes sequential range reads a scan of adjacent pages — great for locality.
