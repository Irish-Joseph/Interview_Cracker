# Daily Interview Prep — January 29, 2026

Five questions worked through this session.

---

### Q1. What is a database connection pool and why is it needed?

**Answer.** Connections are expensive to create (TCP + auth + TLS, tens of ms); a pool keeps N warm connections for the app to borrow/return. Pool size must respect the DB's max_connections — a common outage: 200 app nodes x pool of 20 = 4000 connections vs DB limit 1000. PgBouncer proxies to share physical connections.

---

### Q2. What does idempotency mean and how do you make writes idempotent?

**Answer.** Repeating the operation has no additional effect. Techniques: client-generated idempotency keys deduplicated server-side, PUT (replace, not append) semantics, and unique constraints (re-insert hits the constraint instead of duplicating). Needed for retries over networks where 'did it land?' is unknowable.

---

### Q3. How would you design a short URL service (bit.ly)?

**Answer.** Hash the long URL (or use a monotonic counter / base62 of an auto-increment id) to a 7-char code — counter avoids hash collisions and is cache-friendly. Store code->URL in a KV store or sharded DB, 301 redirect (caches the mapping in browsers) or 302 if you need click analytics. Handle load with a CDN + hot-key replication.

---

### Q4. What is a write-ahead log (WAL) and why is it important?

**Answer.** Every change is appended to a durable log before being applied to the data structures; crash recovery replays the log (redo) and rolls back uncommitted work (undo). It's what makes a database durable after a power loss, and the same log powers replication, point-in-time recovery, and compaction in LSM stores.

---

### Q5. What does ACID stand for and which level do NoSQL systems relax?

**Answer.** Atomicity (all-or-nothing transactions), Consistency (state transitions preserve invariants), Isolation (concurrent transactions don't interfere), Durability (committed data survives crashes). NoSQL systems like Cassandra sacrifice strong consistency/isolation (offer tunable levels, e.g. QUORUM) for availability and horizontal scale.
