# Daily Interview Prep — April 02, 2026

Focus: algorithms. 1 question worked through this session.

---

### Q1. What is dynamic programming, and what makes a problem solvable with it?

**Answer.** Break a problem into overlapping subproblems, solve each once, store the result (memoization/table). Requirements: optimal substructure (optimal solution built from optimal sub-solutions) and overlapping subproblems. Example: Fibonacci, knapsack, longest common subsequence.

---

### Q2. Explain primary key choices: UUID vs auto-increment vs surrogate?

**Answer.** Auto-increment (BIGINT): tiny, fast, cluster-friendly on InnoDB, but predictable (scrapers) and hard to merge shards. UUIDv4: random, no central authority, but 16 bytes scatter B-tree pages (write amplification) — UUIDv7 (time-ordered) fixes locality. Surrogate keys decouple business identity from storage identity.

---

### Q3. What is transactional outbox and why use it?

**Answer.** To keep 'update DB + publish event' atomic: write the event into an outbox table in the same local transaction as the data change, then a relay (polling or CDC like Debezium) publishes it to the broker. Consumers deduplicate (at-least-once delivery) — this is the standard way to avoid 2PC across services.

---

### Q4. What is the STAR method and why do interviewers prefer it?

**Answer.** Situation, Task, Action, Result — a structured story where the interviewer can see context, your specific contribution (Action is where 'I', not 'we', belongs), and a measurable outcome. It forces concrete evidence over generic claims, which is what behavioral screens actually score.

---

### Q5. How do you answer 'Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate'?

**Answer.** Pick a real technical disagreement, show you steelmanned the other view, made the case with data/prototypes, and ended with a decision (yours or theirs) that you executed well. The signal is how you handle being wrong, not that you were right. Avoid 'I never disagree' — it reads as either conflict-avoidant or unengaged.

---

### Q6. How would you estimate a project nobody has built before?

**Answer.** Decompose into known sub-parts, size each (story points or days) with explicit unknowns called out, identify the riskiest item first (spike/prototype it), and present a range with confidence, not a point number. Then name the milestones that would let you re-estimate mid-project.

---

### Q7. Walk through your biggest production bug.

**Answer.** Structure: what it was, how you detected it (monitoring vs user report — be honest), your diagnosis path, the fix, and the systemic change you made so it can't recur (alert, test, runbook, migration guard). The last part is what senior interviewers are actually grading.

---

### Q8. Why do you want to work here / on this team?

**Answer.** Reference something specific you researched: their architecture posts, an open-source project, a product decision you respect, or a stated engineering value. Generic answers ('great company, good culture') signal you sent 50 applications. Match your stated goals to what the team actually needs.

---

### Q9. How do you handle being stuck on a coding problem for 30 minutes?

**Answer.** Say what you've tried and what specifically is blocking, then ask for a nudge (interviewers expect this — silent spinning loses the round). If unsupervised: re-derive from a smaller case, brute-force first then optimize, and always communicate the partial solution and its complexity.

---

### Q10. What does code review actually check for, from a reviewer's view?

**Answer.** Correctness (does it do the spec?), clarity (naming, structure, dead code), tests (are the important paths covered?), and risk (failure modes, rollback, migration safety). As a reviewer, you're also judging the diff's size — a 3000-line PR is itself a process smell worth flagging.

---

### Q11. Tell me about a time you improved a slow system.

**Answer.** Lead with measurement: what the metric was (p99 latency, throughput), how you found the bottleneck (profiling, slow-query log, flamegraph), the fix, and the before/after numbers. 'I added a cache' without the diagnosis story is the answer that loses senior rounds.

---

### Q12. How do you prioritize when everything is urgent?

**Answer.** Impact x urgency vs effort, but first verify the urgency is real (a 'production is down' that's actually a 2% error rate is different). Make the tradeoff explicit to stakeholders rather than silently absorbing it, and leave room for the actual fire when it lands. Then describe how you communicate the deferral.
