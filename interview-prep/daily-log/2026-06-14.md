# Daily Interview Prep — June 14, 2026

Focus: DS + algorithms. 4 questions worked through this session.

---

### Q1. What is the CAP theorem and what do real systems choose?

**Answer.** A distributed system cannot simultaneously guarantee Consistency, Availability, and Partition tolerance — and partitions happen, so the real choice is CP (refuse requests during a partition, e.g. ZooKeeper) or AP (serve stale data, e.g. DynamoDB).

---

### Q2. How does a load balancer work and what are the common algorithms?

**Answer.** It front-ends a pool of servers and routes each connection using round-robin, least-connections, or consistent hashing (minimizes cache invalidation when nodes change). L4 operates on IP/TCP; L7 inspects HTTP (path, headers) and can add TLS termination, retries, and rate limiting.

---

### Q3. Horizontal vs vertical scaling?

**Answer.** Vertical: bigger machine — simple but capped by hardware and a single point of failure. Horizontal: more machines — requires statelessness or sharded/replicated state, load balancing, and network-aware code, but scales past any single box and allows rolling deploys.

---

### Q4. What is sharding and how do you choose a shard key?

**Answer.** Partition data across databases by a key: hash sharding spreads evenly (but range queries hit all shards), range sharding keeps locality (but risks hot ranges). Choose the key from your query patterns — a hot key (e.g. one user's 1B feed) skews load, so you may double-key (user_id, post_id).

---

### Q5. Explain primary key choices: UUID vs auto-increment vs surrogate?

**Answer.** Auto-increment (BIGINT): tiny, fast, cluster-friendly on InnoDB, but predictable (scrapers) and hard to merge shards. UUIDv4: random, no central authority, but 16 bytes scatter B-tree pages (write amplification) — UUIDv7 (time-ordered) fixes locality. Surrogate keys decouple business identity from storage identity.

---

### Q6. What is transactional outbox and why use it?

**Answer.** To keep 'update DB + publish event' atomic: write the event into an outbox table in the same local transaction as the data change, then a relay (polling or CDC like Debezium) publishes it to the broker. Consumers deduplicate (at-least-once delivery) — this is the standard way to avoid 2PC across services.

---

### Q7. What is the STAR method and why do interviewers prefer it?

**Answer.** Situation, Task, Action, Result — a structured story where the interviewer can see context, your specific contribution (Action is where 'I', not 'we', belongs), and a measurable outcome. It forces concrete evidence over generic claims, which is what behavioral screens actually score.
