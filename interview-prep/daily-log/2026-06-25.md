# Daily Interview Prep — June 25, 2026

Focus: design + behavioral. 2 questions worked through this session.

---

### Q1. What does ACID stand for and which level do NoSQL systems relax?

**Answer.** Atomicity (all-or-nothing transactions), Consistency (state transitions preserve invariants), Isolation (concurrent transactions don't interfere), Durability (committed data survives crashes). NoSQL systems like Cassandra sacrifice strong consistency/isolation (offer tunable levels, e.g. QUORUM) for availability and horizontal scale.

---

### Q2. Explain SQL isolation levels.

**Answer.** Read Uncommitted (dirty reads) < Read Committed (no dirty reads; non-repeatable reads possible) < Repeatable Read (same transaction sees the same data; phantom reads possible; InnoDB's default, mostly solved by gap locks) < Serializable (full serializability, via locking or SSI). Each step trades concurrency for correctness.

---

### Q3. What is a sliding window and what does it solve?

**Answer.** Maintain [left, right) over an array with O(1) amortized adds/removes from the ends. Solves fixed-size window max/sum, and variable-size problems like 'longest substring without repeating chars' by shrinking when the constraint breaks.

---

### Q4. Explain quicksort and its worst case.

**Answer.** Pick a pivot, partition around it, recurse. Average O(n log n), in-place, cache-friendly. Worst case O(n^2) on sorted input with naive first/last pivot — fixed by randomized or median-of-three pivot. It's not stable.

---

### Q5. Why is merge sort O(n log n) always, and what does it cost?

**Answer.** It splits in half (log n levels) and each level merges in O(n). Guarantees n log n regardless of input and is stable, but needs O(n) auxiliary space — that's the cost.
