# Daily Interview Prep — July 01, 2026

Focus: behavioral. 3 questions worked through this session.

---

### Q1. Explain a B+tree index.

**Answer.** All data (or row pointers) lives in the leaf level, leaves are linked for range scans, and interior nodes are pure routing keys. Balanced and shallow (3-4 levels for hundreds of millions of rows). The leaf linked list makes sequential range reads a scan of adjacent pages — great for locality.

---

### Q2. What is a deadlock in a database and how do DBs handle it?

**Answer.** Two transactions each lock rows the other needs (T1 locks A waits for B; T2 locks B waits for A). MySQL InnoDB detects via a wait-for graph and kills the victim (ER_LOCK_DEADLOCK); PostgreSQL waits up to deadlock_timeout then detects and aborts one transaction. App fix: consistent lock ordering, short transactions, retry with backoff.

### Q3. What are dirty, non-repeatable and phantom reads?

**Answer.** Dirty read: reading another transaction's uncommitted data (lost if it rolls back). Non-repeatable read: re-reading a committed row that another committed transaction changed. Phantom read: a re-run of a range query returns new rows inserted by another committed transaction. Each maps to the isolation levels above.

---

### Q4. What is MVCC and why do modern databases use it?

**Answer.** Multiversion concurrency control: writes create new row versions instead of overwriting; readers see a snapshot consistent with their transaction start. Readers never block writers and vice versa. Cost: version storage until a vacuum/garbage collector removes versions no snapshot needs (e.g. PostgreSQL autovacuum).

---

### Q5. What's the difference between INNER, LEFT, RIGHT and FULL JOIN?

**Answer.** INNER: rows matching in both tables. LEFT: all left rows, NULLs where no match. RIGHT: mirror of LEFT. FULL: all rows from both, NULLs on either side for non-matches. LEFT JOIN with a WHERE on the right table's non-null column silently becomes an INNER JOIN — a classic bug.
