# Daily Interview Prep — July 13, 2026

Focus: DS + algorithms. 1 question worked through this session.

---

### Q1. How do you handle being stuck on a coding problem for 30 minutes?

**Answer.** Say what you've tried and what specifically is blocking, then ask for a nudge (interviewers expect this — silent spinning loses the round). If unsupervised: re-derive from a smaller case, brute-force first then optimize, and always communicate the partial solution and its complexity.

---

### Q2. What is a foreign key and does it always need an index?

**Answer.** A constraint declaring that a column's values must exist in the parent table's key, with cascade options (RESTRICT/NO ACTION, CASCADE, SET NULL). Most DBs automatically index FK columns (PostgreSQL does; MySQL InnoDB does) because the check needs it — don't assume, check the catalog.

---

### Q3. Explain primary key choices: UUID vs auto-increment vs surrogate?

**Answer.** Auto-increment (BIGINT): tiny, fast, cluster-friendly on InnoDB, but predictable (scrapers) and hard to merge shards. UUIDv4: random, no central authority, but 16 bytes scatter B-tree pages (write amplification) — UUIDv7 (time-ordered) fixes locality. Surrogate keys decouple business identity from storage identity.

---

### Q4. What is transactional outbox and why use it?

**Answer.** To keep 'update DB + publish event' atomic: write the event into an outbox table in the same local transaction as the data change, then a relay (polling or CDC like Debezium) publishes it to the broker. Consumers deduplicate (at-least-once delivery) — this is the standard way to avoid 2PC across services.

---

### Q5. What is the STAR method and why do interviewers prefer it?

**Answer.** Situation, Task, Action, Result — a structured story where the interviewer can see context, your specific contribution (Action is where 'I', not 'we', belongs), and a measurable outcome. It forces concrete evidence over generic claims, which is what behavioral screens actually score.

---

### Q6. How do you answer 'Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate'?

**Answer.** Pick a real technical disagreement, show you steelmanned the other view, made the case with data/prototypes, and ended with a decision (yours or theirs) that you executed well. The signal is how you handle being wrong, not that you were right. Avoid 'I never disagree' — it reads as either conflict-avoidant or unengaged.

---

### Q7. How would you estimate a project nobody has built before?

**Answer.** Decompose into known sub-parts, size each (story points or days) with explicit unknowns called out, identify the riskiest item first (spike/prototype it), and present a range with confidence, not a point number. Then name the milestones that would let you re-estimate mid-project.

---

### Q8. Walk through your biggest production bug.

**Answer.** Structure: what it was, how you detected it (monitoring vs user report — be honest), your diagnosis path, the fix, and the systemic change you made so it can't recur (alert, test, runbook, migration guard). The last part is what senior interviewers are actually grading.
