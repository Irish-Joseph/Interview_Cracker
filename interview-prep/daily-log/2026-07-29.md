# Daily Interview Prep — July 29, 2026

Focus: databases. 1 question worked through this session.

---

### Q1. Explain the divide and conquer pattern.

**Answer.** Split the problem into independent subproblems, solve each recursively, combine the results. The recurrence is T(n) = aT(n/b) + O(n^d); the Master Theorem compares a/b^d to decide the bound. Examples: merge sort, matrix multiplication, closest pair.

---

### Q2. Walk through your biggest production bug.

**Answer.** Structure: what it was, how you detected it (monitoring vs user report — be honest), your diagnosis path, the fix, and the systemic change you made so it can't recur (alert, test, runbook, migration guard). The last part is what senior interviewers are actually grading.

---

### Q3. Why do you want to work here / on this team?

**Answer.** Reference something specific you researched: their architecture posts, an open-source project, a product decision you respect, or a stated engineering value. Generic answers ('great company, good culture') signal you sent 50 applications. Match your stated goals to what the team actually needs.

---

### Q4. How do you handle being stuck on a coding problem for 30 minutes?

**Answer.** Say what you've tried and what specifically is blocking, then ask for a nudge (interviewers expect this — silent spinning loses the round). If unsupervised: re-derive from a smaller case, brute-force first then optimize, and always communicate the partial solution and its complexity.

---

### Q5. What does code review actually check for, from a reviewer's view?

**Answer.** Correctness (does it do the spec?), clarity (naming, structure, dead code), tests (are the important paths covered?), and risk (failure modes, rollback, migration safety). As a reviewer, you're also judging the diff's size — a 3000-line PR is itself a process smell worth flagging.

---

### Q6. Tell me about a time you improved a slow system.

**Answer.** Lead with measurement: what the metric was (p99 latency, throughput), how you found the bottleneck (profiling, slow-query log, flamegraph), the fix, and the before/after numbers. 'I added a cache' without the diagnosis story is the answer that loses senior rounds.

---

### Q7. How do you prioritize when everything is urgent?

**Answer.** Impact x urgency vs effort, but first verify the urgency is real (a 'production is down' that's actually a 2% error rate is different). Make the tradeoff explicit to stakeholders rather than silently absorbing it, and leave room for the actual fire when it lands. Then describe how you communicate the deferral.

---

### Q8. What is the difference between a stack and a queue?

**Answer.** A stack is LIFO — push/pop both happen at the same end; a queue is FIFO — enqueue at the back, dequeue from the front. Stacks model undo, call frames, backtracking; queues model task scheduling, BFS, rate limiting.

---

### Q9. Why does a hash table give O(1) average lookup?

**Answer.** The key is passed through a hash function to index the bucket directly, skipping the scan. It degrades to O(n) when collisions cluster (poor hash or load factor too high), so implementations resize at ~0.7 load and use chaining or open addressing.

---

### Q10. What is a binary search tree and when does it degrade?

**Answer.** A BST keeps left < node < right, giving O(log n) search/insert on average. It degrades to O(n) when the tree becomes a chain (sorted insertions), which is why balanced variants (AVL, red-black) exist.
