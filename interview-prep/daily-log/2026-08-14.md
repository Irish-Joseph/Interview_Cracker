### Q1. How does a CDN work?

**Answer.** An edge network of caches geographically near users, addressed via DNS (CNAME to the CDN, then anycast/nearest-edge selection). Cacheable assets (HTML, JS, media) are served from the edge with cache-control keys; the origin only sees cache misses, cuts latency, and absorbs traffic spikes.

---

### Q2. What is copy-on-write and where is it used?

**Answer.** Share read-only pages between users; on first write, the kernel allocates a new page and copies, then points the writer at it. Used in fork() (child shares the parent's pages until it writes), in ZFS/Btrfs, and in persistent data structures (Clojure's atoms, Rust's Arc-based immutability patterns).
